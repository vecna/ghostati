The genealogy of the read.

Which interventions affected earlier systems, where they stopped working, and what is still untested against today’s pipelines.

How to read this

Each row is a family of face-reading system, oldest first. The heavy part of the line is the years that family was the state of the art. Each mark is one graded entry in the reference archive, placed at the year it was first shown in public — follow a mark to its conditions.

The last two rows are the point: nothing in the archive has been demonstrated against either of them.

2001–2009

Face detection

Haar cascades · Viola-Jones

Rectangles of light and dark slid across the frame. Cheap, fast, everywhere, and it only ever looked for contrast.

3 graded results in the archive.

2010–2013

Landmark geometry

Eigenfaces · Fisherfaces · 68-point meshes

The face as a set of distances between named points. The dome never shows you which way it looks.

Nothing graded in the archive yet.

2014–2016

Learned embeddings

DeepFace · FaceNet · ArcFace

Systems stop measuring features and start learning them: every face becomes a vector in a space nobody designed by hand.

6 graded results in the archive.

2017–2020

Depth & near-infrared

Dot projectors · NIR sensors · liveness

The face as 3D geometry, read in the dark, with a liveness check a photograph cannot pass.

3 graded results in the archive.

2020–now

Networked readers

Pole cameras · shared watchlists

Fixed cameras stop being cameras and become one queryable index. The reading is unchanged; the reach is not.

No documented counter-move in the archive.

2020–now

Wearable readers

Smart glasses · body-worn

The lens moves to eye level and stops looking like a lens. A different technology from the pole, and a different problem: you cannot learn to avoid a camera you cannot identify.

No documented counter-move in the archive.
